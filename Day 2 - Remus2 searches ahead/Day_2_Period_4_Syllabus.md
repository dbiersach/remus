# Day 2, Period 4: Remus2 against Remus1 and the student pair

3:00 to 4:30. Part of [Day 2: Remus2 searches ahead](Day_2_Syllabus.md).

## Goal

The instructor's Remus2 plays the student pair's Remus1 and the student pair, so the class can observe search against random move selection.

## The big picture

The student pair types no code this period. Remus2 is finished in `Day 2 - Remus2 searches ahead/Remus2`, and it passed its tests in Period 3. The student pair's Remus2 plays its Remus1, then the student pair plays together as a team against its Remus2, and then, if time remains, Remus2 plays Stockfish 1500. Tomorrow morning the student pair copies every file of `Day 2 - Remus2 searches ahead/Remus2` into `Day 3 - Remus3 adds the biggest gains/Remus3` and starts Remus3 from those copies, so a student pair that did not finish Remus2 finishes it first.

Lucas Chess runs the student pair's own engines from the day folders. Run the supplied verifier first. Record wins, draws, losses, or unfinished games; a short round illustrates play but does not establish a reliable Elo rating or a controlled speed comparison.

## The period step by step

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 3:00 to 3:25 | Start one round of the student pair's Remus2 against the student pair's Remus1, two 10-minute games played at once in two Lucas workers (Part 4 of the guide), to see what the search buys over a random choice. Beside each engine's score, Lucas Chess shows the depth of its search: Remus2 reports completed iterations in half-moves, and Remus1, which picks a move at random, shows none. Each student pair reads its results tab. |
| 3:25 to 4:05 | The student pair plays together as a team in one 10+0 game against its Remus2: 10 minutes per side, with no increment. The student pair chooses White or Black and agrees on each move (Part 3 of the guide). |
| 4:05 to 4:30, if time remains | One round of the student pair's Remus2 against Stockfish 1500, two 10-minute games played at once in two Lucas workers. |
